# Render critique

## ROUND 1 — initial desktop and tablet render

**Seen in one second:** A serious engineering company, with a clear mission-critical focus and strong brand contrast.

**Idea:** The satellite-to-ground signal path carries through the hero and technical sections rather than becoming a generic decorative motif.

**Live thing:** The four-sector selector changes both the hero image and sector label. Desktop project rows reveal their corresponding image, while touch layouts place the image inline.

**Layout words:** A two-column cinematic desktop hero; a stacked tablet and mobile hero; alternating dark section fields; a two-column capability system; a chronological mission sequence; paired project list and preview; split company statement and principles; direct contact close.

**Scores after revision:** Direction 8/10 · Visual craft 8/10 · Usability 8/10 · Content integrity 9/10.

**Caps and rationale:** No score above 8 for direction, visual craft, or usability. The site has company-published images and no suitable owned mission video, so motion is deliberately limited to image transitions, restrained reveal behavior, and the signal trace. Ascentio does not publish project photography for every named case, so some previews remain contextual company imagery and are labeled accordingly. Contact is routed into the visitor's email application because this repository has no authorized form service or backend.

**Top three problems found and corrected:**

1. The original rocket image carried baked-in lettering, dimmed the hero, and made the lead experience feel less like mission infrastructure. The hero now uses Ascentio's Earth telemetry visual; the rocket remains in the aerospace case context, where its caption is cropped out.
2. The 768px navigation crowded into one row and the hero retained an unbalanced two-column composition. The menu now collapses at tablet width and the hero stacks above the image.
3. Service and contact headings wrapped into awkward three-line blocks, and the service section left a large dead zone. Both headings have intentional line breaks, and section spacing is tighter.

**Current limits:** No honest company-owned footage surfaced in the published pages reviewed. The four sector images are Ascentio-published and their photographer/licence is not specified on the source pages; project cards therefore describe them as sector context rather than evidence of a particular delivery. The contact form prepares an email draft and does not submit to a server.

## FINAL REVIEW — 1440 / 1280 / 768 / 390px

The final browser inspection reports no horizontal overflow, broken images, or console errors at any requested width. The hero heading holds at two lines at every viewport. The only remaining inspector note is the deliberate absence of video; the page's motion remains lightweight and supports navigation and reading.
